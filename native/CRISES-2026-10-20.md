# National crises and published statistics (2026-10-20, 0.9.101)

**The request (player):** "I destroyed every enemy building except its headquarters and its GDP was still stronger than
mine." The statistics should look like the economic and military figures that are really published about countries;
research what else is published and put it in the game. And: damage to infrastructure and buildings should be able to
throw a country into a crisis, economic, political or diplomatic.

## Why GDP stayed high

`world_stats.gd` computed `GDP = real-world GDP × (0.55 + 0.45 × output / output at the first release)`. A rival starts
with only its headquarters, which was not counted, so its first output was 0 (taken as 1): whatever it built multiplied
its GDP many times over, and when everything was destroyed the 0.55 floor still left it with more than half of its
real-world economy. With the United States or China as the rival, that is more than most players' whole economy.

**Now:** GDP = real-world GDP × (what stands / a normal developed economy) × how hard it works.

- *What stands* (`crisis.gd`, `_measure`): every finished non-military building by its cost, a civic one at 0.6, each
  scaled by its health (a damaged factory works less), plus 4 for the capital's own ministries and streets.
- *A normal developed economy*: 45 (a capital and some fifteen to twenty working buildings, reached by a rival after
  about six to ten minutes). A nation starts below its real figure and reaches it as it builds; above it, returns
  diminish (the square root), so a sprawling economy is not many times the real one.
- The capital counts 4. Growth is measured over a quarter (three minutes) against at least half the real economy, so
  the first factories of a young economy are not a thousandfold boom.
- *How hard it works* (`activity`): blackouts (up to -35%), a currency crisis (-10%), default (-15%), a political crisis
  (-7%), a fallen government (-15%), condemnation (-3%), war (-5%), sanctions.

There is no floor: in the test a developed rival's GDP falls from $23.6 tn to $3.0 tn when its power stations, farms,
homes and industry are destroyed.

## What the sources say

**Damage, losses and needs.** The World Bank, UN and EU damage assessment for Ukraine (RDNA5, to December 2025) separates
**physical damage** (about $195 bn: housing, transport and energy hit hardest) from **economic losses** (about $667 bn:
lost commerce, industry, public services and livelihoods). Losses are several times the damage: the game's crises
model exactly that multiplier, where a lost power station costs not its price but the output it powered.
(https://www.undp.org/ukraine/publications/ukraine-fifth-rapid-damage-and-needs-assessment-rdna5-february-2022-december-2025)

**Strikes on energy.** Ukraine's ministry of economy estimated output down about 30% over the first nine months of the
war, and a fall of 39% in the month the strikes on its power system began, with blackouts of six hours or more a day;
a later estimate put the energy strikes' cost at about one point of growth in a year.
(https://gmk.center/en/news/the-fall-in-gdp-in-october-accelerated-to-39-due-to-attacks-on-the-energy-facilities-the-ministry-of-economy/amp,
https://mind.ua/en/news/20249337-ukraines-gdp-decline-may-reach-almost-40-in-2022-due-to-prolonged-power-outages-ministry-of-econ,
https://ukrinform.net/rubric-economy/3919659-russian-strikes-on-ukraines-energy-objects-decrease-gdp-growth-rate-german-economist.html)
→ an **energy crisis** at 40% of energy capacity lost: production -15%, research -10%, GDP down up to a third.

**From shortages to the fall of a government.** In Sri Lanka a lack of foreign currency meant no fuel, rolling
blackouts of up to 13 hours, mass protests, a curfew and the cabinet's offer to resign; the economic crisis became a political one.
(https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/sri-lanka/politics-2022.htm,
https://kathmandupost.com/world/2022/04/01/sri-lanka-imposes-curfew-after-protests-over-economic-crisis-turn-violent)
→ the chain **blackouts and prices → pressure → political crisis → the government falls**; the fall waits for 90
seconds of protest.

**War, inflation and credit.** Wars move inflation and bond yields more than pandemics; rating agencies downgrade
sharply; a sovereign default is followed by lower trade (about 8%, for years), banking crises, and devaluations raise
the odds of a change of leader.
(https://journals.umcs.pl/h/article/view/14419, https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/a-moodys-ratings-downgrade-for-the-us-what-now)
→ **currency crisis** past about 30% inflation; **sovereign default** when the implied rating collapses; default costs
trade (income -12%) and raises inflation.

**What is published.**
- IMF World Economic Outlook: real GDP growth, consumer-price inflation, unemployment, current account, general
  government net lending and **gross debt as a share of GDP**. (https://imf.org/external/datamapper/datasets/WEO/1)
- SIPRI Military Expenditure Database: military spending in dollars, **as a share of GDP**, per head and as a share of
  government spending. (https://www.sipri.org/node/222)
- Fragile States Index: 12 indicators in four groups (cohesion, economic, political, social), each 0 to 10, a total of
  0 to 120. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fragile_States_Index)
- UNHCR counts refugees and the internally displaced; credit-rating agencies publish sovereign ratings.

The baselines in `crisis.gd` (inflation, unemployment, debt, military spending, fragility) are round recent figures
from these publications; they are data, not text the player reads.

## In the game

### Statistics (Diplomacy > Statistics)

| Group | Indicator | For other nations |
|---|---|---|
| (first) | **Overview**: every nation's GDP, growth, inflation, unemployment, debt, **credit rating**, fragility, and its crises | public, a release late |
| Economy | GDP, **growth** (over a quarter, 3 minutes; below zero drawn), **inflation**, **unemployment**, **public debt** | public, a release late |

### The crises (`scripts/crisis.gd`)

Measured every two seconds for every nation. The measure of "normal" settles on what stands over six minutes (an economy
learns to live with its losses); an economy smaller than 20 is measured as 20 (a young nation is not thrown into
depression by one farm); a crisis begins only when its cause has held six seconds (a building being replaced is not a
crisis); what you **demolish** yourself is a choice, not a loss.

| Crisis | Begins | Ends | You | A rival |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Recession | output down 15% | below 8% | income -6%, happiness -3 | income ×0.94 |
| Depression | output down 40% | below 25% | income -15%, production -5%, happiness -6 | ×0.85 |
| Energy crisis | energy capacity down 40% | below 20% | production -15%, research -10%, income -5%, happiness -4 | ×0.90 |
| Food crisis | farms and fisheries down 50% | below 25% | happiness -5, health -4 | ×0.96 |
| Currency crisis | inflation past 30% (or its usual + 25) | below 20% (usual + 12) | income -8%, happiness -3 | ×0.92 |
| Sovereign default | two minutes of depression or currency crisis, an implied rating below C, debt past 100% (and 50 points over its usual), 15 points below where it began | rating back | income -12% | ×0.88 |
| Displacement | 15% of the people without a home | below 7% | happiness -4, income -3%; UNHCR appeals | ×0.97 |
| Political crisis | pressure 25 | below 15 | income -6%, production -5%, war support drains | ×0.93 |
| Government collapse | pressure 45 after 90 s of protest (and not within 5 minutes of the last) | 3 minutes | a democracy or hybrid regime **signs a ceasefire** in all its wars; an autocracy **cracks down** (no peace; democracies think less of it) | ×0.85 |
| International condemnation | a striker destroys homes, towns, power, farms, hospitals and schools of another (weights 1 to 3; 6 points) | under 2 (a point a minute fades) | every nation neither at war with you nor allied: relations -8 (the victim gains 3); income -4% | ×0.96 |

Indicators: inflation, unemployment and defence spending follow targets set by the losses, war and sanctions; public
debt grows with the deficit (war, reconstruction, defence) and jumps when the economy shrinks; pressure (fragility above
its usual level) comes from losses, blackouts, hunger, inflation, joblessness, displacement, default, condemnation and a
weary home front (an autocracy feels 80% of it); the implied rating (AAA to D) from debt, inflation, fragility, losses
and war.

Every crisis also drains the people's support for the war (war_support.gd), which already makes a rival take peace
sooner and refuse new wars. Your crises are messages that begin "CRISIS:" (filed under the economy in the situation
report) and end with "Recovery:"; a rival's are "World news:" (public news: depression, blackouts, a currency crisis,
default, displacement, protests, a fallen government, condemnation; at most one every 40 seconds).

Test: `tools/crisis-check.gd` (49 checks). `tools/stats-check.gd` follows the new GDP.
